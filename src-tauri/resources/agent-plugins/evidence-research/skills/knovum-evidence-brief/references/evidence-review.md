# Reviewing evidence

Read for comparison, disputed claims, or a consequential recommendation.

Record claims before composing the conclusion. Prefer direct evidence from the relevant period. Two sources copying the same assertion are one evidence lineage, not corroboration. Preserve units, dates, denominators and scope; do not compare unlike measurements.

For each disagreement check definition, period, method, population and incentives before attributing it to error. Separate absence of evidence from evidence of absence. Confidence reflects source quality and agreement, not the number of paragraphs you wrote.

Choose criteria from the user's decision. Mark unknowns as unknown and explain which could change the recommendation. Cite the current source revision and exact locator. Quote only what is needed. The conclusion must not be stronger than its weakest material premise.

Before delivery: trace every material statement to the ledger, check numbers against originals, include meaningful opposing evidence, remove unsupported causal language, and state actionable remaining questions.
